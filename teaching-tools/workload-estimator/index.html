Course Workload Estimator

Estimate how many hours students will spend on your course, and compare the total with a credit-hour standard. Many US institutions expect about 45 hours of student work per credit; set the number your institution uses.

Course info

45 hours per credit matches the US federal credit-hour definition (one hour of instruction plus two hours of outside work per week for about 15 weeks) and many state and system policies. Check your institution's policy for its number and for how long a "class hour" is.

How the estimates work

Reading

Pages per hour = (words per minute × 60) ÷ words per page. Reading speeds by purpose and difficulty:

Purpose; new conceptsWords/min450 w/p600 w/p750 w/p

Writing (hours per page)

Genre; drafting250 words500 words

Writing time is assumed to grow in a straight line with length. Reflection takes half as long as argument, and research takes twice as long. The original authors call the writing estimates far more speculative than the reading estimates.

Credit-hour benchmark

The benchmark is hours of work per credit × credits. 45 is the common US figure: the federal definition (34 CFR 600.2) describes a credit hour as one hour of direct instruction and at least two hours of out-of-class work each week for about 15 weeks, and many state systems state it as 45 hours per credit with a 50-minute class hour (for example, Arizona Board of Regents Policy 2-224). Institutions differ, so both numbers are editable. The status shows "on target" within ±10% of the benchmark.

Assumptions added for this prototype

  • Media time multipliers (×1, ×1.5, ×2).
  • Discussion posts: text at 333 words per hour; audio/video at 3× runtime.
  • Exams: 5 study hours per exam by default.
  • Term-length presets and the 45-hour, 50-minute defaults.

These are starting values, not research findings. Change them to fit your course.
